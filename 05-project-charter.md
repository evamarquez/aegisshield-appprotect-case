# Document 05 - Project Charter

**Project:** AegisShield AppProtect  
**Stage authorized:** Product definition and technical validation  
**Status:** Simulated sponsor approval  
**Sponsor:** CTO

## Authorization

The CTO authorizes the Project Manager to coordinate a limited product-definition and technical-validation stage for AppProtect. This authorization does not approve production protection or a broad product launch.

## Business need

AegisShield wants to evaluate a second product that can complement its SIEM. The first version must solve a clear customer problem without creating a new source of service interruption or unsafe blocking.

## Objective

Prepare enough product, technical, operational, financial, and customer evidence for the sponsor to decide whether to authorize a controlled BookEasy pilot.

## Approved boundary

The team may define and validate AppProtect for one BookEasy web application. It may assess an existing security engine, create a visual prototype, define rules and exceptions, estimate resources and costs, and prepare pilot controls.

The team may not deploy production protection, roll out to other customers, build a proprietary engine, or redesign the existing SIEM.

## Deliverables

- Product brief and scope.
- Requirements and acceptance criteria.
- Administrator journey and prototype.
- Technical option and licensing assessment.
- Stakeholder, communication, and kickoff records.
- WBS, RACI, backlog, estimates, budget, Gantt, and capacity view.
- Test, risk, data-protection, monitoring, and recovery plan.
- Pilot-readiness recommendation and sponsor decision.

## Methodology

The project uses a hybrid approach:

- Planned controls for scope, approvals, risks, budget assumptions, dates, and release gates.
- Short discovery and design loops for interviews, prototype feedback, technical checks, and requirement changes.

In simple terms, the team agrees on the limits first, then learns and improves the first version in small steps.

## Initial milestones

| Milestone | Target |
|---|---|
| Charter and first-release scope approved | Week 2 |
| Prototype and requirements reviewed | Week 4 |
| Technical and cost options assessed | Week 5 |
| Pilot test plan and controls ready | Week 7 |

## Authority

- The CTO approves scope, budget boundary, major risks, and the decision to authorize pilot build work.
- Product owns customer value, first-release priorities, and acceptance scenarios.
- Engineering owns technical feasibility, engine evaluation, estimates, and implementation options.
- Security owns security controls, data protection, and rule-safety review.
- Operations owns monitoring, support, recovery, and handover requirements.
- The Project Manager coordinates the plan, records decisions, tracks risks, and escalates blockers.

## Resources and budget assumption

The fictional plan assumes part-time availability from Product, Engineering, Security, Design, Operations, QA, Customer Success, and the Project Manager. Budget figures will be labeled as simulated planning assumptions and will be reviewed by the sponsor before any build authorization.

## Success criteria

The stage succeeds when the sponsor receives a clear scope, evidence of a real customer problem, a feasible technical path, a safe test approach, an initial cost and schedule view, and a recommendation with conditions.

## Key terms

**Charter:** The document that authorizes the work and sets its limits.

**Gate:** A review point where the sponsor decides whether work can move forward.

**Pilot build:** A limited build prepared for controlled customer testing.

**Release approval:** Permission to put a product change into an agreed environment.
